<VisuallyHidden asChild><h2>Intro</h2></VisuallyHidden>

<ComponentHeader title="Navbar" tier="Organism" />

The application header: a named banner at the top of the page holding the brand, the primary navigation
and a place for the actions that belong to the whole app (search, an account menu, a call to action).
Below a width you choose, the navigation leaves the bar and is offered in a side panel behind a menu
button, so a phone gets a list it can reach with a thumb. A compound component, meaning it is made of
named sub-parts you compose together: `Navbar.Brand`, `Navbar.Links` holding `Navbar.Link` items,
`Navbar.Actions`, and `Navbar.MenuButton`, which is drawn for you whenever there are links.

### Import

```tsx
import { Navbar } from "@dbm-design-system/components";
```

## Properties

`Navbar` is the `<header>` element, so it also accepts any other native `<header>` attribute beyond
what's listed here (e.g. `onFocus`, `title`), passed straight through. `dir` is set by the bar and can't
be replaced by a same-named attribute. Parts are found among the bar's direct children (the automatic
menu button looks for `Navbar.Links` and `Navbar.MenuButton` there); a part wrapped in a fragment or
another component still draws, but isn't noticed.

<PropertiesTable of={NavbarRootStories} order={propOrder} />

### Navbar.Brand properties

The logo and name, linking home. Also accepts any other native `<a>` attribute beyond what's listed
here (e.g. `onClick`, `title`), passed straight through.

<PropertiesTable of={NavbarBrandStories} order={brandPropOrder} />

### Navbar.Links properties

The primary navigation: a `<nav>` holding a list of `Navbar.Link`s. Also accepts any other native
`<nav>` attribute beyond what's listed here. Its children are drawn twice, in the bar and in the menu
panel, so give them no `id`.

<PropertiesTable of={NavbarLinksStories} order={linksPropOrder} />

### Navbar.Link properties

One link of the navigation, an `<li>` holding the `<a>`. Everything below, and any other native `<a>`
attribute beyond what's listed here, goes on the `<a>`.

<PropertiesTable of={NavbarLinkStories} order={linkPropOrder} />

### Navbar.Group properties

A link that opens more links: a dropdown in the bar, an expandable group in the menu panel. Also accepts any
other native `<button>` attribute beyond what's listed here (e.g. `onFocus`, `title`), passed straight
through to its button. Its children are `Navbar.Link`s.

<PropertiesTable of={NavbarGroupStories} order={groupPropOrder} />

The bar's end. Also accepts any other native `<div>` attribute beyond what's listed here.

<PropertiesTable of={NavbarActionsStories} order={actionsPropOrder} />

The button that opens the menu panel below the collapse width. Also accepts any other native `<button>`

## Variants

### Variants

`outlined` is a surface with a line under it, `filled` a tinted surface, and `ghost` nothing of its own,
for a bar that sits over a hero or a coloured page.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.Variants} />

### Marking the current page

`currentVariant` chooses how the current link is marked: `underline` (the default) is a brand line under the link
(a bar at the start edge in the phone panel), `subtle` a tinted pill, and `solid` a brand fill. It is one look for the
whole bar, the phone panel and a `Navbar.Group` that holds the page included, and can't be set per link.
`aria-current="page"` is set whichever it is, and a link is never told apart by colour alone. The underline grows in when a link
becomes the current one, and only then: not on a first paint, and not when the menu panel opens on a link already current.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.CurrentVariants} />

### Rounded links

`rounded` makes each link and group button a pill, as it does on `Button`, `IconButton` and `Toolbar`. It is the
links that round (and the focus ring and the current link's tint or fill with them); the bar keeps its shape.
It reads best with `subtle` or `solid`, since an underline has no shape to round.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.Rounded} />

### Sizes

The bar is as tall as an `IconButton` of the same step plus room above and below, so a button in the bar
never touches its edges.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.Sizes} />

### Search and an account menu

Whatever belongs to the whole app goes in `Navbar.Actions`. An account menu is a `Menu` whose trigger is
an `IconButton`; it opens above the bar, even a sticky one.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.WithActions} />

### Links that open more links

A `Navbar.Group` is a button in the bar that opens a floating `Menu` of its links, with the arrow keys,
typeahead and `Escape` a menu has. It sits in the list with the other links and is drawn like them.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.WithGroup} />

### A group in the menu panel

On a phone a floating list is awkward, so the same group is an expandable one: its button is
`aria-expanded` and shows the links indented beneath it, without closing the panel. A group that holds the
current page (`current`) starts expanded. Choosing one of its links closes the panel, as the plain links do.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.GroupInThePanel} />

### Contained

`contained` keeps the content to the page's reading width, centred, while the bar's surface still spans
the page.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.Contained} />

### The menu panel

Below the collapse width, the same `Navbar.Link` items fill a side panel (a `Drawer`) behind the menu
button, and choosing a link closes it. This demo collapses below the widest step so it shows on any
window narrower than 1920px.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.CollapsedMenu} />

### Where it collapses

`collapseBelow` takes a breakpoint step, or `"never"` to keep the links in the bar at every width. The
choice is made by the stylesheet, so a server-rendered page is right at first paint and a window dragged
across the width switches without any script.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.CollapseStep} />

### Sticky

`sticky` keeps the bar at the top as the page scrolls, with a surface behind it and a shadow once content
has moved beneath it. In a scrolling box of your own, `scrollContainerRef` names the box.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.Sticky} />

### Current page and a disabled link

`current` marks the page the reader is on with `aria-current="page"` and a tinted look; you decide which
link it is, from your router. A `disabled` link blocks its click and stays in the tab order.

<Canvas of={NavbarStories.CurrentPage} />

## Usage guidelines

<div className="dbm-do-dont-grid">
<Callout tone="success" title="Do">

- Use one `Navbar` per page, at the top, for the links a person needs on every screen.
- Keep the primary links few (five or six) and put the rest in a `Menu` or a footer.
- Mark the current page with `current`, from your router, so it is right on a server-rendered page.
- Put app-wide controls (search, notifications, the account menu) in `Navbar.Actions`.
- Use `sticky` for an app people scroll inside; leave it off for a short marketing page.
- Pass the navigation a name when the page has more than one (`aria-label` on `Navbar.Links`).
- Use `asChild` with your router's link so navigation stays client-side.

</Callout>
<Callout tone="danger" title="Don't">

- Don't use a `Navbar` for the actions of an editor or a table: that is a `Toolbar`.
- Don't use it for a persistent navigation column: that is a `Sidebar`.
- Don't give `Navbar.Link` an `id`: the same items are drawn in the bar and in the menu panel.
- Don't put more than one `Navbar` on a page unless each has its own `aria-label`; they are all banner
  landmarks.
- Don't hide the menu button or set `collapseBelow` so low that a phone shows a bar of links that does not
  fit.
- Don't fill the bar with controls that need the arrow keys (a text field with a menu inside): it is a row
  of ordinary tab stops, not a toolbar.

</Callout>
</div>

## Best practices

- The bar is a `<header>` (a banner landmark) holding a `<nav>`. Your page's skip link belongs before
  it, pointing at the `main` landmark; the bar doesn't own that target (see the recipe below).
- Links are ordinary tab stops, in reading order: brand, links, actions, then the menu button.
- The collapse follows the window width, so a narrow window on a desktop gets the same menu a phone does.
  If the panel is open when the window grows past the step, it closes.
- A link that opens more links is a `Navbar.Group`: a `Menu` in the bar and an expandable group in the panel. Keep a group to a handful of
  links; a wide panel of grouped links, with descriptions or images, is a mega-menu, which is `NavigationMenu`.
- `menuSide="start"` is the right edge in right-to-left text. Pass `dir` on the bar and it reaches the
  panel too, which is drawn in a portal and would otherwise follow the page.
- Use `labels` to translate the menu button, the navigation's name and the panel's close button.

## Accessibility

<Callout tone="info" title="A banner landmark holding a named navigation, with the current page marked">

- The root is a `<header>`, which is a banner landmark when it is not inside an article or section.
  `aria-label` names it when a page has another header.
- `Navbar.Links` is a `<nav>` with a name (`labels.navigation`, "Main", unless you pass `aria-label`)
  holding a `role="list"` list; each link is a `role="listitem"`, which keeps list semantics under
  Safari with VoiceOver, where a list without markers loses them.
- The current page is `aria-current="page"`. A disabled link is `aria-disabled` and stays focusable, with
  its click blocked.
- Keys: `Tab` and `Shift+Tab` move through the brand, the links, the actions and the menu button in order;
  there are no arrow-key shortcuts, because this is a navigation and not a toolbar. `Enter` follows a
  link.
- A `Navbar.Group` in the bar is a button with `aria-haspopup="menu"` and `aria-expanded` that opens a menu: `Enter`, `Space` and
  `ArrowDown` open it, the arrow keys and typeahead move through the links, `Escape` closes it and focus returns to the button. In the
  menu panel it is a disclosure button with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` pointing at its list of links, which are out of the tab
  order and the accessibility tree while it is collapsed.
- The menu button is an `IconButton` named "Menu" with `aria-expanded` and `aria-haspopup="dialog"`. The
  panel is a modal dialog named "Menu" with focus held inside; `Escape`, the scrim and the close button
  close it, and focus returns to the menu button.
- Below the collapse width the bar's own links are `display: none`, so they leave the tab order and the
  accessibility tree; the panel's copy is what a screen reader finds.
- Every control is at least 24 by 24 pixels. The focus ring is the standard two-pixel outline in
  `border.focus`.
- The motion is a short colour fade on hover, and the underline growing in when a link becomes the current one (from the link's start edge in the bar, from its top in the menu panel), both removed under `prefers-reduced-motion`, where the line is simply there. In
  forced-colours mode a filled or stuck bar and the current link get a one-pixel outline, since a fill or
  shadow alone would vanish.
- `subtle` uses `text.brand` on `bg.brand-subtle`, the pairing `Tabs` uses; `solid` uses `text.on-brand` on `bg.brand`, the pairing
  a primary `Button` uses, with its focus ring drawn inside the link in `icon.on-brand`; `underline` keeps `text.primary` and draws a
  `bg.brand` line. In forced colours the underline is a system-colour line and the other two get an outline.

</Callout>

## Code examples

```tsx
<Navbar sticky>
  <Navbar.Brand href="/">dbM</Navbar.Brand>
  <Navbar.Links>
    <Navbar.Link href="/products" current>
      Products
    </Navbar.Link>
    <Navbar.Link href="/pricing">Pricing</Navbar.Link>
    <Navbar.Link href="/docs">Docs</Navbar.Link>
  </Navbar.Links>
  <Navbar.Actions>
    <Button size="sm">Sign in</Button>
  </Navbar.Actions>
</Navbar>

{/* Collapse later, and keep the page's own width */}
<Navbar collapseBelow="lg" contained>
  …
</Navbar>

{/* A router's links */}
<Navbar.Link asChild current>
  <RouterLink to="/products">Products</RouterLink>
</Navbar.Link>

{/* A dropdown in the bar, an expandable group in the phone panel */}
<Navbar.Links>
  <Navbar.Link href="/products">Products</Navbar.Link>
  <Navbar.Group label="Resources" current={onResources}>
    <Navbar.Link href="/blog" current={path === "/blog"}>Blog</Navbar.Link>
    <Navbar.Link href="/guides" current={path === "/guides"}>Guides</Navbar.Link>
  </Navbar.Group>
</Navbar.Links>

{/* A button drawn as a link: it leaves the phone panel open, where a link closes it */}
<Navbar.Link asChild>
  <button type="button" onClick={openSearch}>Search</button>
</Navbar.Link>

{/* A skip link, before the bar: it jumps to the page's main landmark */}
<a href="#main" className="skip-link">Skip to content</a>
<Navbar>…</Navbar>
<main id="main">…</main>

{/* Open the menu from your own state */}
<Navbar menuOpen={open} onMenuOpenChange={(next, { reason }) => setOpen(next)}>
  …
</Navbar>

{/* A native <header> attribute, passed straight through */}
<Navbar aria-label="Site header" title="dbM">
  …
</Navbar>
```
